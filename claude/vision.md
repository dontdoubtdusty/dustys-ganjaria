# Vision

## What it is
A time-management shop game set in Dusty's Ganjaria, a legal cannabis dispensary. A customer comes to the counter with an order, you prepare it across a few stations, one screen each, and get a score for speed and accuracy. Tips buy unlocks. It is a solo project for the owner only.

It is an original game in the order-and-stations genre. It borrows the genre's structure, not any existing game's name, characters, art, interface, look or code.

## Full design (owner's answers, 2026-11-17, four question rounds)
**Familiarity comes first.** A fan of the Papa's series should sit down and know how to play within a minute: a dispensary twist on that genre. That means the genre's whole familiar flow: take the order at the counter, ticket on the rail, stations on tabs, each station a hands-on minigame, per-station points revealed at hand-over, tips, rank-ups that unlock content, named regulars with favourite orders, shop days with rushes. Every series entry has its own stations, and so do we: ours come from dispensary work. What stays our own in every specific: names, characters, art, interface layout and code (nothing taken from any Papa's game file).

- Session: shop days. A clock runs from opening to closing (about 6 real minutes) with a quiet morning and busier lunch and evening rushes. At closing the doors shut, you finish whoever is inside, then a day summary, then a save. Rejected: endless shift, fixed-count challenge runs, story weeks, even flow, fixed customer count per day.
- Pressure: cozy but busy. Walkouts happen but are rare if you keep moving; care is rewarded more than raw speed. Rejected: hard juggling, no fail state.
- Stations: Counter (take the order and check ID), Weigh (flower) or Roll (pre-rolls), Package (container, label, hand-over). Three screens per order, four stations in all. Later: a closed-shop late-night session (trimming or similar), designed when we get there. Rejected: ID as its own screen; every order weighed then rolled; 2 or 5+ stations.
- Tickets: a ticket rail along the top of every screen; drag a ticket onto a station to work on it. Several orders can be in progress at different stations at once. Rejected: automatic oldest-first (what 0.4.0 does), click to pick, one order at a time.
- Menu: flower only on day 1; pre-rolls and the Roll station unlock after a few days. Rejected: everything from day 1, edibles at the start.
- Judging: per-station points (wait, weigh or roll, package), shown one by one at hand-over and averaged into the order score. Rejected: one overall score, stars only.
- Progress: tips buy shop upgrades (faster needle, bigger jar, comfier waiting area that slows patience drain, decor) and cosmetics (uniform, shop colours, signs); order points raise the shop rank, which unlocks content (pre-rolls, strains, stations, customers).
- Customers: named regulars with favourite orders and their own patience, plus random walk-ins. New regulars appear as you rank up.
- ID check (at the Counter): glance and decide. The customer holds out an ID; check birth date, expiry and photo, accept or refuse. Most are fine, a few a day are not. Rejected: ID only on suspicion, no ID check.
- Package: drop the sealed bag into the right container (pouch or jar), drag the label on straight (scored on angle and position), hand it over at the counter. Physics like the weigh station. Rejected: label only, one paper bag per order.
- Roll (when unlocked): toss ground flower into a cone up to a fill line, tap to pack, twist the tip with a timing ring. Rejected: grind then roll.

  - Hand-over uses the same over-the-shoulder shot: the customer reacts to each station's points as they count up.
  - Our own layout and art throughout; only the camera convention is shared with the genre.
  - Reference for the two camera shots only (official genre screenshots, never traced or copied): `claude/reference/genre-shop-view.jpg`, `claude/reference/genre-order-shot.jpg`.
  Rejected: front-on first-person counter (what 0.4.0 does).

## Weigh station redesign: tongs and tray (designed 2026-11-25 with the owner, built in 0.16.0, not played yet)
Grounded in research on Colorado deli-style counters (`claude/research-colorado-budtending.md`). Replaces tossing buds into a bag on the scale. The 2026-11-17 "dragging single buds" decision stands; what changes is how the nug is handled, where it goes and what counts as right.

Owner's calls (three question rounds):
- **Tongs, drag and drop.** Press on a nug: the tongs close on it (a squeeze, a pluck sound). Carry it; it sags and lags behind the tongs more the heavier it is, so a big nug feels heavy. Let go over the tray. Nugs can be carried back from the tray to the jar the same way.
- **A weigh tray on the scale, tipped into the bag.** Nugs go onto a tray on the scale plate. When the weight is right, grab the tray's handle and carry it over the open bag; it tilts as it passes over the mouth and the nugs slide in. Moving too fast bounces a nug out onto the counter (pick it up with the tongs and put it in the bag). Then zip and throw into the OUT bin as now.
- **Lopsided target.** Under the order weight fails (a steep accuracy drop). From the order weight up to the weigh-heavy cap is full marks. Over the cap, accuracy falls as it does today. The cap is the order weight plus 6% or plus 0.15 g, whichever is more: 1 g allows 1.00-1.15, 3.5 g allows 3.50-3.71, 7 g allows 7.00-7.42. The dial shows this band in green and the zone below the weight in red.
- **Free grams cost the shop.** Grams over the order weight in each shipped bag, plus crumbs, add up to a day-end results line, "Given away: 1.4 g ($14)", taken off the day's tips like an ID fine. A generous bag earns the customer's tip a small bonus, smaller than its cost. The trade-off is time against money: landing near the weight takes more swaps (Waiting, patience); landing heavy is quick but costs.
- **No breaking nugs.** The cap exists so nobody has to. Every jar is filled so that whole nugs can land in the band (see Fill below).
- **Size is the only reading.** No number readout on the scale, no weight hint on the nug. Size already matches weight; players learn each strain by eye.
- **Crumbs from hard drops and fumbles.** Letting a nug fall from high above the tray, or flinging it, chips a crumb onto the counter: the crumb's weight leaves the nug, counts toward the giveaway, and costs a little handling. A nug that misses the tray lands on the counter and costs handling, as spills do now. Gentle set-downs are the skill the tongs teach.
- **Some customers ask for looks.** Now and then a ticket says "Big nugs" or "No shake" (also said in the order shot). The bag check at hand-over looks for it: met earns a smile and a tip bonus, missed a frown and a smaller tip. Scores do not change.

Chosen by Claude as defaults (tune after playing):
- Layout: jars on the left as now; the scale with the tray in the middle; the open bag next to the scale; the OUT bin on the right. Built differently: the bag already stands open on the counter when the ticket arrives (no bag stack, one step fewer), since the tray carry is the new step.
- Crumb rule: a release more than about 60 px above the tray surface, or faster than a set speed, chips 5% of the nug (at least 0.02 g). Crumb handling cost 5; a nug on the counter 10 (as now).
- Under the weight: accuracy reaches zero at one band-width below the order weight. Over the cap: zero at four band-widths, as now.
- Giveaway price $10 a gram (placeholder; the game has no prices yet). Tip bonus for a heavy bag: 25% of the giveaway's value.
- Fill: after filling a jar, check (on a 0.01 g grid) that some set of its whole pieces lands in the band; if not, add small pieces until one does. `SmallSpare` stays.

## Station mini-games (owner, 2026-11-26: build all four; built in 0.20.0)
Design round (owner picked Claude's recommended option on all four, 2026-11-26):
- Heat seal: one hold per pouch, no redo; handling scored on how close to the middle of the green it was let go (100 in the middle, 80 at the band's edges, 0 at 0.36 away). Gaps under, scorch over.
- Bag drop: the bag slides on an overhead rail over the container (Claude's call: a trolley on a rail, not a pendulum, so the timing reads). A miss lands on the table, counts as a drop and is hoisted back; a 7 g bag over a small container rests on the rim. Clicking anywhere drops (Space too); a wrong container is swapped by dragging another onto the mat.
- Label slap: one slap, final, placement scored only on distance from the guide (straightness dropped). The slide speeds up 10% a rank (260 to 460 px/s). Another pad can be picked before the slap.
- Label slap, reworked in 0.21.0 (owner, 2026-11-26: the click did not feel like slapping; owner picked hand slam + peel + smooth from Claude's list): pick a pad, peel the backing by dragging its tab 130 px, then the label slides as before and curls (flat 0.7 s, full 2 s later); click and a hand slams it on (smack, station shake, container jolt). Curl sets the bubbles (one per 20%, a lifted corner from 50%), rubbed flat with the button held (0.16 s each, corner 0.3 s); the order is done when flat. Claude's call: bubbles come from the curl, not from placement, so a bad slap is not punished twice and waiting for a perfect pass has a cost. Peeling and rubbing cost only time (label par 2 s to 3.5 s); a peeled label swapped for another pad is tossed. Placeholder sounds (`slap`, `peel`, `rub`) made in code until the owner picks recordings.
- Lid notch: 3 good clicks; a miss counts as a cross-thread (25 handling) and gives no turn; the notch speeds up 22% after each good click.
- Claude's calls: Packing shares moved from container 50 / speed 30 / handling 20 to 40 / 25 / 35, so the closing skill matters; handling is the seal (100 on jars) less 25 per missed notch and 10 per drop. Label placement full within 6 px and zero at 50 px (was 2 and 30 for dragging); label par 1.5 s to 2 s. Pack pars unchanged (7 s pouch, 9 s jar).

Earlier plan:
The genre turns station steps into small abstract skill games (land on the green, stop the marker) instead of literal drag-and-place. Rule: abstract the steps that take time but hold no skill or decision; keep the ones that already are a skill or a decision. Planned, in Claude's suggested order (Pack first: it is mostly drag chores):
1. **Pouch heat seal** (replaces sweeping the strip): hold the sealer, a heat bar climbs, let go in the green. Too short leaves gaps, too long scorches. Real heat sealers work this way.
2. **Bag drop** (replaces dragging the bag into the container): the bag swings over the open container; click to drop it as it passes the mouth.
3. **Label slap** (replaces carry, stick and press): the label slides back and forth over the container; click to slap it on, scored on distance from the marks. Also fixes labeling being too easy.
4. **Jar lid notch** (replaces the sideways strokes): a rotating notch, click as it lines up, a few times.
Kept literal: weighing (the dial already is land-on-the-green, and the tongs are the game's signature), the zip and the throw (already skills), the container pick and the ID check (decisions). Each needs its own design round with the owner (speeds, scoring, what a miss costs) before it is built; the play log and mechanics page change with each.
