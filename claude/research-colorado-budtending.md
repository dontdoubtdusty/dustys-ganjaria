# Research: Colorado deli-style budtending (2026-11-25)

Done to ground the weigh station redesign (vision.md, "Weigh station redesign"). First-party sources where possible: Colorado's Marijuana Enforcement Division rules (1 CCR 212-3, Cornell LII copy, version effective 2024-12-04; currency today not confirmed), the state auditor, the legislature's handbook, and the shops' own pages. Flowhub (a dispensary point-of-sale company) is an industry source, not a shop.

## What the sources say
- Two ID checks: before entering the restricted access area, and again before the sale (Rule 6-110(B)).
- At most 1 oz of flower or its equivalent per transaction (8 g concentrate = 1 oz); same-day sales count together when the employee knows or should know (6-110(C)).
- Product may only be displayed inside the restricted access area, never visible from outside (6-110(J)).
- The container's label carries license numbers, harvest batch number, net weight, the universal symbol, total THC and CBD %, "Flower, shake, or trim", date of sale, warnings, and a use-by date no more than 9 months after harvest (3-1010, 3-1015).
- A container that is not child-resistant goes into a child-resistant exit package at the point of sale (3-1010).
- Seed-to-sale tracking through the point of sale; the store must be able to reconcile on-hand inventory with the system (3-810).
- Any sale may be refused (6-110(D)). Responsible Vendor training: at least 2 hours, 70% to pass, covers impairment, tracking compliance, hours of sale (3-515, 3-520).
- Commercial scales must be inspected every 12 months (2024 state audit). No MED rule found that names the store's counter scale.
- Life Flower: flower "in huge glass jars on the shelves"; budtenders "bring flower jars over" to see bud structure and smell; "weighed to order right in front of them at the register".
- Star Buds: flower "hand-selected and weighed live in front of you".
- JARS (The Deli): "Choose your strain, select your quantity... weigh it out fresh"; a jar to smell before choosing; 1 g to an ounce (per Herb).
- Flowhub: at a deli counter customers "can usually look at and smell the product but are not allowed to touch it". Risks: "slowing down check out, labeling containers in-store, accidental spillage, and overweighing". A "weigh heavy threshold" lets the budtender weigh heavy "and not force the budtender to break up big nugs"; example: an eighth weighed at 3.7 g, the extra 0.2 g free. Shake: small pieces that fall off nugs, sold cheaper.

## Findings
1. The target is lopsided: under the weight is the hard line; over is allowed up to a shop's weigh-heavy cap, and the extra is given away.
2. Nobody breaks nugs; the cap exists so they do not have to. The skill is choosing which nugs make the weight.
3. Customers look and smell, never touch; the budtender brings the jar.
4. At a deli counter weighing and labeling go together: the label carries the weight the scale showed.
5. The industry's own failure list (slow checkout, labeling in store, spillage, overweighing) maps onto ways a player can fail.

## Not confirmed
First-hand budtender accounts (Reddit was not reachable); nugs per eighth, time per weigh, tools (tongs come from Leafly's general advice); which rule covers the counter scale; whether the rules are current today.
